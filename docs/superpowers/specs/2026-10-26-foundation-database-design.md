# Foundation Database Design (M1, v2)

Status: approved by the user, 2026-10-26. This replaces the schema section of
`2026-08-30-foundation-module-design.md`. The architecture, layering, and
English-only term mapping in that file still apply.

## Why

Foundation's first schema had a `users` table that staff filled in. The user
has since decided how people really join the church app:

- People register themselves, one household at a time, from a link staff send
  them. Staff do not type member records, and nobody can register without a
  link, so there are no spam sign-ups.
- One adult registers, then adds the rest of the household. Family members can
  then claim their own login, or never log in and let an adult act for them.
- Families span branches, and the family tree outlives households.

This schema is what every later module joins against (worship check-in,
events, ministry, finance, library), so it has to get people, households, and
access right first.

## Goals

- Every person has their own record and a permanent, printable Member ID.
- Any adult in a household can act for anyone else in it.
- A family tree that survives children moving out to start their own household.
- Login, OTP, password reset, and brute-force rules from `CLAUDE.md`.
- Branch isolation, with one deliberate, read-only, audited exception for
  household members.

## Non-goals (this module)

- Branch transfer requests and their two approvals: Membership (M2).
- Merging duplicate people: later, in Membership.
- Baptism, sidi, marriage records, death records: later modules.
- Content translation tables: Worship (M3), with the warta.
- The full groups feature: Worship extends the `groups` stub.

## Anti-goals

- A person's data visible to anyone outside their branch except household
  members' branches, read-only.
- NIK in clear text in audit rows, list queries, or logs.
- A registration link that can be used twice.
- Revealing whether an email, phone, or account exists, in login, activation,
  or password reset.

## Decisions made by the user

1. **Self-registration by link.** Staff create a registration link. It is
   single use: one household registers with it, then it is dead. It never
   expires.
2. **Household flow.** The registrant adds spouse and children. For each, the
   registrant may enter an email or phone number. That person later activates
   their account: enters that email or phone, proves it with an OTP, and sets a
   password. A person with no email or phone (a young child) never logs in;
   adults act for them.
3. **Acting for others.** Any adult in a household can open the data of anyone
   in the same household and act as them. Whether this still applies to an
   adult who has activated their own account is a church-wide setting
   (`system_settings`), on by default. The audit log records both the actor and
   the person acted for.
4. **Moving out.** When a child marries and starts a household, they leave the
   parents' household completely. Parents can no longer open their account.
   The family tree still links parents, child, and grandchildren.
5. **Home branch.** Each person has exactly one home branch. Members of one
   household may have different home branches.
6. **Cross-branch view.** Staff can view the full record of anyone in the same
   household as one of their branch's members. They cannot edit it; only the
   person's home-branch staff can.
7. **NIK** (16-digit national ID) is optional. When given, it is unique. It is
   masked on screen (`3174••••••••0001`).
8. **Member ID** is given to every person for their member card.
9. **IDs** are UUIDs on every table.
10. **Person details:** sex, Chinese name (optional), member photo, and a full
    address (street, postal code, city).
11. **Files** (photos, documents) live in one polymorphic `attachments` table
    (`owner_type`, `owner_id`). A new feature adds files without a new table or
    column.
12. **Church photos:** a branch can have several photos, all shown.
13. **NIK is encrypted** at rest.

## Calls I made (check these)

- **Adult = age 17 or over** (KTP age), computed from `birth_date`, which is
  required. A constant in code, not a setting.
- **UUID version 7** for every primary key, generated by Postgres `uuidv7()`.
  New rows sort by creation time, so keyset pagination can order by `id` alone
  and indexes stay compact. This needs **Postgres 18**; `docker-compose.yml`
  moves from `postgres:16-alpine` to `postgres:18-alpine`. The 18 image mounts
  its volume at `/var/lib/postgresql`, not `/var/lib/postgresql/data`; change
  both services and drop the old `churchapp_pgdata` volume (no live data).
- **Member ID format:** `<branch code>-<year>-<5-digit number>`, e.g.
  `JKT-2026-00042`. Branch code and year are those at registration, and the ID
  never changes, even after a transfer. Numbers are never reused.
- **People and accounts are two tables.** `people` holds who someone is;
  `accounts` holds how they log in. A child without login has no account row.
  Secrets stay out of every people query.
- **No `member` role row.** Every registered person is a member of their home
  branch through `people.home_branch_id`. `role_assignments` holds staff roles
  only. "Guest" means not logged in.
- **NIK encryption:** AES-256-GCM in `nik_encrypted`, plus `nik_hash`, an HMAC
  of the NIK, for the unique index and lookups. Both keys come from env through
  `config.ts`. NIK stays out of list queries, audit diffs, and logs.
- **Sex** is a Postgres enum (`male`, `female`), not a table: the list is fixed
  and nobody edits it.
- **Nickname and birth city** are added; they are used on check-in lists and
  church certificates. Occupation, education, baptism, sidi, previous church,
  and death date belong to Membership (M2) in its own `member_profiles` table.
  Marital status is never stored; spouse rows in the tree already say it.
- **One current address per person.** Past addresses live in the audit log.
- **Soft delete only (user's rule).** Rows holding church data are never
  deleted. They get `deleted_at timestamptz` instead: `branches`, `groups`,
  `households`, `people`, `person_relationships`, `role_assignments`,
  `registration_links`, `attachments`. Repositories filter
  `deleted_at is null` by default, and every unique index on these tables is
  partial `where deleted_at is null`, so a replaced photo or removed role does
  not block a new one. Foreign keys are `restrict`. Removing a row is an update,
  so it is audited like any change.
- **Exceptions, hard-deleted by design:** `sessions` (logout must end them),
  `otp_codes` and `login_throttles` (short-lived security data), and
  `error_logs` (1-month retention). None of them is church data.
- **Accounts are never removed;** `status = suspended` turns one off.
- **Polymorphic attachments** were the user's preference over per-owner tables
  or one FK column per owner. The lost foreign keys are covered by the registry
  and the owner check on write.
- **Edit conflicts:** two adults can edit the same record. Updates compare
  `updated_at` and reject a save if the row changed since it was loaded.
- **Branch codes never change** once a Member ID uses them.
- **Postgres runs with a UTF-8 locale,** so the trigram name search also
  matches Chinese characters.
- **Registration links can be revoked** by staff (a link sent to the wrong
  number). Not expiring was the user's call; revoking is mine.
- **Cross-branch views are audited.** Each time staff open a person from another
  branch, an audit row (`person.viewed_cross_branch`) records who looked at whom.
- **Who creates links:** `branch_admin` for their branch, `superadmin` for any.
- **Staff are people too.** A staff member registers like anyone else and gets
  roles on top. The first superadmin comes from the seed script, with a
  household of one.
- **Moving out** is started by the person themselves (17 or over) or by staff.
  Divorce uses the same move.
- **Shared phone:** an email or phone belongs to one account. A wife who uses
  her husband's phone has no login of her own; adults act for her.
- **Location:** normalized into `countries` → `regions` → `cities`; a person
  stores only `city_id`. Every country uses pick lists, so there is no typed
  city. The location is per person (a child studying in Australia) and
  required. The street and postal code are typed. A city missing from the dataset is added by a
  developer to the seed, not typed in by users.
- **Finance stays branch-strict.** The cross-branch exception covers personal
  data only, not giving history. Finance (M7) designs its own access.

### `people`

`city_id`, trigram on `full_name`, `nickname`, and `chinese_name` (`pg_trgm`,
for the searchable select), unique `member_code`, partial unique `nik_hash
where nik_hash is not null`.

### `attachments`

Every file in the app: member photos, church photos, and later documents,
event posters, book covers.

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | uuid | |
| `owner_type` | enum `attachment_owner`: `person`, `branch` | later modules add values |
| `owner_id` | uuid | the owner's `id` in that table |
| `purpose` | enum `attachment_purpose`: `profile_photo`, `church_photo` | later modules add values |
| `position` | smallint | display order within owner and purpose, 0 first |
| `storage_key` | varchar(255) | unique; where the bytes live, never a URL or full path |
| `content_type` | varchar(100) | from an allow list (`image/jpeg`, `image/png`, `image/webp`, `application/pdf`) |
| `size_bytes` | integer | |
| `sha256` | char(64) | of the content |
| `uploaded_by_person_id` | uuid | FK people |
| `created_at` | timestamptz | |

- Index `(owner_type, owner_id, purpose, position)`: loads every attachment of
  a page of owners in one `WHERE owner_type = $1 AND owner_id IN (...)` query,
  never one query per row.
- Unique `(owner_type, owner_id, purpose, position)`. One profile photo per
  person: partial unique `(owner_id) where purpose = 'profile_photo'`.
- A branch can have many church photos, all shown in `position` order; 0 is
  the cover.
- A new feature adds an enum value (`ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE`), never a table
  or column.

The database cannot check `owner_id` with a foreign key, so code does two
jobs instead. Orphans cannot appear, because owners are never deleted (soft
delete only), so there is no cleanup job.

1. **One registry** in `modules/attachment/` maps each `owner_type` to its
   access check (`person` → `canViewPerson()`, `branch` → anyone, guests
   included). An owner type with no entry is denied. Files are served only
   through one endpoint that runs this check; never from a public folder, and
   the PWA never caches them offline.
2. **Writes check the owner exists** in the same transaction as the insert.

### `countries`, `regions`, `cities` (location reference data)

Country 1 → many regions (province or state) 1 → many cities.

| Table | Column | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `countries` | `id` | uuid |
| | `iso2` | char(2), unique, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 (`ID`, `AU`, `CN`) |
| | `name` | varchar(100), English name (`Australia`) |
| `regions` | `id` | uuid |
| | `country_id` | FK countries, not null |
| | `code` | varchar(10), nullable, official code (`31` for DKI Jakarta) |
| | `name` | varchar(150) (`DKI Jakarta`, `New South Wales`) |
| `cities` | `id` | uuid |
| | `region_id` | FK regions, not null |
| | `code` | varchar(10), nullable, official code (`31.71`) |
| | `name` | varchar(150) (`Kota Jakarta Pusat`, `Sydney`) |

- Unique `(country_id, name)` on regions and `(region_id, name)` on cities.
  Indexes on `regions.country_id`, `cities.region_id`, and a trigram index on
  `cities.name` for the searchable select.
- Seeded once, read-only in the app. Indonesia comes from the official
  Kemendagri region codes (provinces, then kota and kabupaten). Other countries
  come from the open countries-states-cities dataset (ODbL licence; its credit
  goes on the About page).
- The screen shows the country name in the reader's locale with
  `Intl.DisplayNames` from `iso2` (`澳大利亚`). Region and city names stay as
  stored; they are proper nouns.

### `member_code_counters`

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `branch_id` | uuid | FK branches, part of PK |
| `year` | smallint | part of PK |
| `last_number` | integer | not null |

Primary key `(branch_id, year)`, no `id`. The next Member ID comes from
`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE SET last_number = last_number + 1 RETURNING`
inside the registration transaction, so two registrations never get the same
number.

### `person_relationships` (the family tree)

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | uuid | |
| `kind` | enum `relationship_kind`: `parent`, `spouse` | |
| `person_id` | uuid | FK people, restrict. For `parent`: the parent |
| `related_person_id` | uuid | FK people, restrict. For `parent`: the child |
| `started_on` | date | nullable, spouse only (wedding) |
| `ended_on` | date | nullable, spouse only |
| `created_at` | timestamptz | |

Checks: `person_id <> related_person_id`; for `spouse`, `person_id <
related_person_id` so each couple is stored once. Unique `(kind, person_id,
related_person_id)`. Indexes on `person_id` and `related_person_id`.

Households and the tree are independent: moving out changes `household_id`
only, never these rows. Grandchildren are parent rows two steps away.

### `accounts` (login)

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `person_id` | uuid | PK, FK people |
| `email` | varchar(255) | nullable, unique, stored lowercased |
| `phone` | varchar(20) | nullable, unique, stored E.164 (`+62812...`) |
| `password_hash` | varchar(255) | null until activated |
| `activated_at` | timestamptz | null until activated |
| `last_login_at` | timestamptz | nullable |
| `status` | enum `account_status`: `active`, `suspended` | default `active` |
| `created_at`, `updated_at` | timestamptz | |

Check: `email is not null or phone is not null`. The row is created when the
registrant (or any adult in the household) enters an email or phone for a
person. Changing or resetting the password, or suspending the account, deletes
every session of that person in the same transaction.

### `login_throttles`

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `identifier_hash` | char(64) | PK, HMAC of the normalized email or phone typed |
| `failed_count` | smallint | not null, default 0 |
| `locked_until` | timestamptz | nullable |
| `updated_at` | timestamptz | |

Counts failures per identifier typed, whether or not an account has it, so an
unknown email locks exactly like a real one and the lockout reveals nothing.
After `LOGIN_MAX_FAILED_ATTEMPTS` (env, default 5), `locked_until = now() + 2
minutes`. A success deletes the row. A scheduled job deletes rows idle for a
day. No `id`: the hash is the key.

### `otp_codes`

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | uuid | |
| `person_id` | uuid | FK people, cascade |
| `purpose` | enum `otp_purpose`: `login`, `activation`, `password_reset` | |
| `channel` | enum `otp_channel`: `whatsapp`, `email` | |
| `code_hash` | char(64) | HMAC-SHA-256 of the 6-digit code with a server key; plain SHA-256 of 6 digits is reversible |
| `challenge_hash` | char(64) | unique, hash of the token in the step-2 cookie |
| `attempts` | smallint | default 0; the code dies after 5 wrong tries |
| `expires_at` | timestamptz | 5 minutes after creation |
| `consumed_at` | timestamptz | nullable |
| `created_at` | timestamptz | |

Index `(person_id, purpose, created_at)`: serves "one password reset per 2
minutes", "at most 3 codes per person per purpose per 15 minutes", and finding
the live code. Sending a new code kills the previous live one. The challenge hash ties step 2 to the
browser that passed step 1. A scheduled job deletes rows older than 1 day.

### `sessions`

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | char(64) | PK, SHA-256 of the cookie token (not a UUID; it is a hash) |
| `person_id` | uuid | FK people, cascade: who logged in |
| `acting_as_person_id` | uuid | FK people, set null: who they act as, null = self |
| `expires_at` | timestamptz | |
| `created_at` | timestamptz | |

Index `person_id` (revoke all sessions of a person). Switching to act as a
household member updates `acting_as_person_id`; the service checks the
household rule on every request, not only at the switch, so moving out ends
access at once.

### `registration_links`

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | uuid | |
| `token_hash` | char(64) | unique, SHA-256 of the token in the link |
| `branch_id` | uuid | FK branches: the registrant's home branch |
| `created_by_person_id` | uuid | FK people: the staff member |
| `note` | varchar(255) | nullable, who it was for (`Budi, sent by WA`) |
| `used_at` | timestamptz | nullable |
| `household_id` | uuid | FK households, nullable: set when used |
| `revoked_at` | timestamptz | nullable |
| `created_at` | timestamptz | |

Single use is enforced in SQL: registration runs `UPDATE registration_links
SET used_at = now(), household_id = $1 WHERE token_hash = $2 AND used_at IS
NULL AND revoked_at IS NULL RETURNING id` in the registration transaction. No
row returned means the link is dead and the transaction rolls back. Index
`(branch_id, created_at)` for the staff list of links.

### `role_assignments`

Unchanged apart from `user_id` becoming `person_id` and `member` leaving the
enum: `superadmin`, `pastor`, `branch_admin`, `group_admin`. Checks: superadmin
has no branch or group; pastor and branch_admin have `branch_id` only;
group_admin has `group_id` only. Unique `(person_id, role, branch_id,
group_id)` with `NULLS NOT DISTINCT`. Index `person_id`.

### `system_settings`

| Column | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | uuid | so audit rows can point at it |
| `key` | varchar(100) | unique |
| `value` | jsonb | not null |
| `updated_by_person_id` | uuid | FK people, nullable |
| `updated_at` | timestamptz | |

One key for now: `household.adults_act_for_activated_adults`, boolean, default
`true` (seeded). Code reads settings through one zod map of known keys, so an
unknown key or wrong type fails. Changes are audited. Only superadmin edits.

### `audit_logs`

As today, plus:

- `actor_user_id` becomes `actor_person_id`: who was logged in.
- New `on_behalf_of_person_id` (FK people, set null): the person acted for,
  null when acting as self.
- New index `(branch_id, created_at)` for a branch's activity page.

The diff helper drops `password_hash` and replaces any NIK change with `{ nik:
{ changed: true } }`. Actor columns are `set null` only for accounts removed by
a developer; people are only soft-deleted, so history keeps every name.

## Access rules (enforced in services, read from these tables)

- **Branch scope:** `getAccessibleBranchIds()` is unchanged and still filters
  every branch-owned query.
- **Cross-branch view:** one function, `canViewPerson(target)`, allows a read
  when the target shares a household with any person whose home branch is in
  the caller's accessible branches. Edits never use it. Each allowed read from
  outside the caller's branches writes `person.viewed_cross_branch`.
- **Acting for:** allowed when actor and target share a household, the actor is
  17 or over, and either the target has no activated account or the setting is
  on. Checked on every request.

## Flows the schema must support

1. **Register:** staff create a link (row in `registration_links`). The
   registrant opens it and submits their details plus their email or phone and
   password. One transaction: claim the link, create the household, the
   person, their Member ID, and their account; audit it. Then they log in with
   an OTP as usual.
2. **Add a household member:** an adult adds a person to their household with
   the same home branch by default, and may pick another. Optional email or
   phone creates an unactivated account row. Parent and spouse rows go into
   `person_relationships`.
3. **Activate:** the person enters their email or phone. If an unactivated
   account matches, an `activation` OTP is sent; otherwise the screen behaves
   the same and sends nothing. After the code, they set a password.
4. **Move out:** the person gets a new household; their tree rows stay.
5. **Login and reset:** as in `CLAUDE.md`, using `accounts` and `otp_codes`.
