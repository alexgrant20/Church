# Major North American ChMS: Planning Center, Breeze, Pushpay ChMS (CCB), ACS Realm

Research date: 2026-10-27. Many vendor help pages returned 404/403 or loaded only with JavaScript (Pushpay support is a Salesforce portal; Capterra and G2 block fetches). Where a finding comes from a search-result summary of a page rather than a full fetch, it is marked "(search snippet)". Third-party "review" blogs (ChurchMemberPro, CostBench, TheLeadPastor, ChurchStack) are often affiliate or competitor marketing. Treat their claims as secondary.

## Q1. Modules, pricing model and target church size

### Takeaway
Planning Center is modular: a free People database plus separately priced apps (Services, Check-Ins, Groups, Giving, Registrations, Calendar, Church Center). Breeze is one flat price (about $72/month) with unlimited people and is now sold under the Tithely brand. Pushpay ChMS (formerly CCB) and Realm are quote-based. Both target mid-to-large and multi-site churches. Realm is the only one of the four with built-in fund accounting and payroll.

### Cited Findings

**Planning Center (PCO)**
- People is free for every church, with an unlimited database and reporting. There are no contracts. — [PCO pricing](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing); [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)
- Each other product has a free tier and is then priced by its own usage metric:
  - Services: free up to 5 team members, then priced by team members.
  - Registrations: free up to 5 attendees at the largest event, then priced by attendees.
  - Giving: free up to 10 donations a month, then priced by monthly donations.
  - Check-Ins: free up to 10 Sunday check-ins, then priced by Sunday check-ins.
  - Groups: free up to 15 group members, then priced by people in groups.
  - Calendar: free with 1 room. Paid plans add room and facilities management.
  - Church Center (the member app and web platform) is free. A paid upgrade adds custom pages, themes, sermon notes and audio/video channels.
  - Music Stand is a $5/month add-on.

  Tier prices were not shown on the fetched page. — [PCO pricing](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing)
- Other costs:
  - Texting from People costs $0.02 per text, bought as credits.
  - A toll-free texting number costs $5/month.
  - US giving fees are 2.15% + $0.30 per card transaction and $0.30 per ACH transfer.

  A church that goes over a plan limit keeps working, and PCO emails an upgrade suggestion after repeated overages. — [PCO pricing](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing)
- Reviewers say a large church can approach $1,000/month. One third-party estimate puts a larger church using all apps at $200–500+/month. — Software Advice and other reviews summarized via search: [Software Advice PCO reviews](https://www.softwareadvice.com/accounting/planning-center-profile/reviews/), [ChurchMemberPro PCO review](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/planning-center-review/) (search snippet)
- Comparison blogs call PCO the common default for churches that want a modular system. They regard its worship and service planning (Services) as the strongest in the category. — [FlockConnect comparison](https://blog.flockconnect.com/comprehensive-church-management-software-comparison-2025/) (search snippet; vendor-adjacent)

### Inferences
- PCO's model puts one free, shared people record at the center. Each ministry app (Services, Check-Ins, Groups, and so on) reads from it and is billed on its own usage metric, so cost grows with the ministry. Breeze bundles everything at one flat price. CCB and Realm sell enterprise bundles by quote.
- Only Realm has a full double-entry ledger linked to giving and membership.

- Current PCO tier prices were not visible on the fetched pricing page; it likely renders them through an interactive calculator.
- Current official Realm and Pushpay prices are not published.

## Q2. People and membership: records, custom fields, households, relationships, intake, directory, export

### Takeaway
None of the four has a true family tree. PCO and Breeze model households only. PCO's own help says there is no built-in field for family relationships; churches use a custom field. Realm adds person-to-person "relationship tags" (e.g. Godparent, Neighbor) shown on both profiles and queryable in reports, but it has no tree view. Realm and CCB use family positions (Head, Spouse, Child, Other, or Primary Contact) that control who can edit family members and their privacy. Intake runs through forms that feed workflows (PCO Workflows, CCB Process Queues/Workflows, Realm Pathways, Breeze follow-ups).

### Cited Findings

**Planning Center People**
- Profiles bring together attendance, giving and volunteering. A profile also holds:
  - notes for prayer requests, counseling and health conditions
  - email and text history, kept for up to 3 months
  - background check ordering, with completion and expiry tracking
  - duplicate merging and CSV import — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)
- Custom fields live on custom tabs; a church must create a tab before it can add fields. Only managers can add them.
  - Field types: text, paragraph, date, yes/no, dropdown, checkboxes and number.
  - A dropdown field cannot later be changed to a checkbox field.
  - Example uses: baptism date, spiritual gifts, communication preferences. — [PCO help: manage custom fields](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/manage-custom-fields.html) (search snippet); [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)
- Households:
  - A person can belong to multiple households (e.g. children of divorced parents).
  - Parents and guardians in a household can check in children and register for signups on their behalf.
  - Adult children or relatives who need those abilities can be set as "other adults". — [PCO help: households](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/households.html) (search snippet; page now 404)
- Relationships: "there isn't a default field in People to store family relationship information, but you can save these details in a custom field." — [PCO help: common uses for custom fields](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/common-uses-for-custom-fields.html) (search snippet)
- Lists:
  - Lists are built from rules matching all, any or none of the conditions, and they refresh automatically.
  - They can be organized by name, campus, category or star.
  - Staff can message a list by text, email or Church Center announcement. — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)
- Intake and follow-up:
  - Forms add respondents to workflows based on their answers. Examples: prayer requests, volunteer interest, visitor contact details.
  - Workflows are multi-step, for goals such as membership or serving.
  - Automations trigger follow-up after event signups or baptism class completion. They also update profiles on changes (age, membership status, primary campus).
  - Welcome emails are sent after a connection card is completed.
  - Tasks are created automatically, e.g. ordering a background check after a volunteer signup. — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)
- Church Center profile self-service:
  - Admins choose which profile fields members see.
  - Each field can be editable, editable only if blank, or view-only.
  - A search snippet reports a limit of 20 custom profile fields in Church Center. — [PCO help: set up Church Center in People](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/set-up-church-center-in-people.html) (search snippet)
- Directory:
  - The directory is invite-only. Members choose which details they share, including for household members. "No information will be shared without consent from you or someone in your household."
  - Members can choose whether others may contact them, and can remove themselves and their household with "Remove all".
  - A privacy mode hides emails and phone numbers. Members then use a contact button, and replies go to the sender's primary email. — [PCO help: share information in the directory](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/share-information-with-people-in-the-directory.html) (search snippet; page now 404)
- Export:
  - Org admins and managers can export CSVs.
  - Viewers and editors can export only with "Can export CSVs and reports" checked. This setting was added in January 2025; before that, anyone with People access could export. It is off by default for new users.
  - Private custom fields can be exported only by people with access to those fields.
  - Each list has an export button. Exports over 500 rows are emailed as a link.
  - A full-database export is under gear menu > Import/Export CSV.
  - Accounts has a separate CSV of product users and their permissions. — [PCO help: export data](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/export-data.html); [PCO help: permissions in People](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/permissions-in-people.html) (search snippets)
- Security: two-step verification, per-user permission levels, and a People mobile app for staff. — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)

**Breeze**
- Families are a data object. The People API covers members, contacts, custom profile fields and families, with tags as a separate resource. — [Breeze People API (unofficial spec)](https://apis.io/apis/breeze-chms/breeze-chms-people-api/) (search snippet)
- Member churches ask people to fill in the "Family" section of their Breeze profile. — [First UU Nashville](https://firstuunash.org/news/lets-connect) (search snippet)
- Tags are the core way to segment data: admins filter and segment the database with a simple tag system. Breeze also tracks household relationships. — [TheLeadPastor / ChurchStack reviews](https://www.churchstack.io/reviews/breeze) (search snippet)
- Forms, a member directory, automation and follow-ups are included in the plan. — [Breeze pricing](https://www.breezechms.com/pricing)
- Free data export is advertised. — [Breeze pricing](https://www.breezechms.com/pricing)

**Pushpay ChMS (CCB)**
- Profile types are Basic User and Limited Access User. Statuses are pending, active and inactive. Pending profiles have restricted access until an admin approves them. — [Pushpay support: profile statuses and types](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/Basic-and-Limited-Users) (search snippet)
- User types on the legal page:
  - Limited Access User: cannot see any people.
  - Basic User: can see other listed individuals.
  - Small Group Leader: can see the people and data in their groups.
  - Custom admin roles: over 100 configurable areas. — [Pushpay CCB legal](https://pushpay.com/legal-center/ccb-legal/) (search snippet)
- Privacy: each member picks Listed or Unlisted. An unlisted person is visible only to church leadership. — [CCB basic user guide (church, 2018)](https://gmzaustin.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Basic-User-Guide.pdf) (search snippet; older)
- Children's profiles below an age threshold are visible only to:
  - the Primary Contact and Spouse in the family
  - People admins
  - people with active "Child Work Approved" status — [CCB glossary](http://designccb.s3.amazonaws.com/helpdesk/files/official_docs/glossaryofterms.pdf) (search snippet; older)
- Saved searches and reports can include Family Position and Campus. Unlisted and "Child Protected" people are excluded by default from some reports. — [Churchteams CCB export guide](https://help.churchteams.com/knowledge/how-to-export-church-community-builder-ccb-information-to-a-csv-file-for-importing-into-churchteams) (search snippet)
- Process Queues are structured multi-step workflows, e.g. a welcome program or volunteer training.
  - Setup: create the process, optionally add a process manager, add queues as steps, then configure each queue's General, Details and Automation tabs.
  - Profiles enter a queue in 6 ways: automatically when marked done in a prior queue, manually, from a profile, from a form response, from People Search, or from certain reports.
  - From People Search, admins can bulk-add a filtered list to queues and set a queue manager, a status or a note.
  - Notes have per-note visibility: "Appropriate leaders", "People admins", or "Only me". — [Pushpay support: Process Queues](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/Getting-Started-Process-Queues); [How to add profiles to a queue](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/How-To-Add-Profiles-to-a-Process-Queue); [Notes](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/Getting-Started-Notes) (search snippets)
- Profiles also have family connections, custom fields, giving history, an activity timeline, advanced search and duplicate merging. — [ChurchMemberPro CCB review](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/ccb-church-community-builder-review/)

**Realm**
- Profiles:
  - Personal, family and contact details, giving history, volunteer info, and private Pastoral/Staff notes.
  - Unlimited custom profile fields, with control over labels, attributes and access.
  - Sacraments tracking (Catholic and Protestant) and Special Instructions (e.g. allergies).
  - A change log, duplicate detection, and automatic data validation and normalization.
  - Skills and interests, plus a full view of each person's serving, groups and event attendance.
  - Organization by campus. — [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)
- Self-service: members update their own profiles, custom fields, family and communication preferences. Staff can lock certain fields to staff-only editing. — [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)
- Family positions:
  - "Head" and "Spouse" are primary positions. They can edit family members' contact and personal info, change their privacy, and opt them into the directory.
  - "Child" and "Other" cannot.
  - "That person will be notified by email" when another family member changes their privacy.
  - Removing a family member's profile needs an admin. — [Realm help: your family](https://help.acst.com/realm/how-to/profiles/manage-your-information/your-family)
- Relationship tags link two individual profiles. Both people must have a profile.
  - Each side gets its own drop-down label, and the link shows on the Relationships tab of both profiles.
  - Admins can create, rename, merge and delete unused tags. Defaults exist; examples are Godparent and Neighbor.
  - Staff report on them through Reporting > Custom Query filtered by Relationship and tag, e.g. "all grandparents" for an event.
  - The help page describes no tree or graph view, and no enforced reciprocity such as parent and child. — [Realm help: relationship tags](https://help.acst.com/en/realm/how-to/profiles/track-relationships/relationship-tags)
- Privacy levels can apply to the whole profile or field by field:
  - Anyone in the church: directory opted-in members only; not available for under-18s.
  - Leaders & group/serving team members.
  - Leaders.
  - Users with permission only.
  - Custom Privacy: a level per field.

  Staff with permission always see profiles. Anyone with a login can see every other login holder's name and picture. — [Realm help: your family](https://help.acst.com/realm/how-to/profiles/manage-your-information/your-family)
- Staff can set privacy defaults by member status and assign role-based access for staff and lay leaders. — [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)
- The online photo directory is built from member-authorized profiles and searchable by name. — [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)
- Pathways track progress through steps such as membership class, baptism or ministry involvement. — [ChurchTechToday 2017](https://churchtechtoday.com/chms-review-acs-realm/) (older)
- Import is CSV. The Profiles page did not mention export. — [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)

### Inferences
- The industry norm is households or families plus a position or role. Realm's relationship tags are the closest thing to a family graph among these four, and even Realm has no tree view.
- Supporting real family trees (parents, grandparents, in-laws across households) would set a product apart.
- Directory privacy is consent-driven everywhere. Realm's per-field, per-audience levels (leaders, my groups, whole church, staff only) are the richest. PCO adds "household consent" and contact-without-revealing-email. CCB uses Listed/Unlisted plus age-based child protection.
- Export is permission-gated. PCO only recently (January 2025) separated the export permission from view access, which suggests that leaking data through exports was a real concern.

### Gaps
- No official Breeze help article was retrieved on family roles, custom field types, directory privacy or export permissions. support.breezechms.com did not appear in search, and features pages now redirect to Tithely.
- Pushpay support articles are a JavaScript portal and were not readable, so CCB family-position definitions are only known from older docs.
- None of the sources showed whether PCO's household model records a role per member (e.g. a head of household) beyond adult and child.

## Q3. Communication: email/SMS and list targeting

### Takeaway
Each product targets messages through saved, dynamic segments: PCO Lists (rule-based, auto-refreshing, filterable by campus), Breeze tags and filters, CCB saved searches and groups, and Realm groups and queries. SMS is metered in PCO and Breeze. Realm leans on a Constant Contact integration for email.

### Cited Findings
- PCO: rule-based Lists (all/any/none conditions) can be messaged by email, text or Church Center announcement. Email templates are available. Texting costs $0.02 per text, plus $5/month per toll-free number. — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people); [PCO pricing](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing)
- PCO keeps email and text history on the profile for up to 3 months. — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)
- Breeze includes unlimited email, a drag-and-drop editor, and 250 texts a month (then $10 per 500). Tags segment recipients. — [Breeze pricing](https://www.breezechms.com/pricing); [ChurchStack Breeze review](https://www.churchstack.io/reviews/breeze) (search snippet)
- CCB/Pushpay offers email and US SMS only, with no WhatsApp and no multilingual interface. — [ChurchMemberPro CCB review](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/ccb-church-community-builder-review/)
- Realm Communications integrates with Constant Contact. The Connect app supports group messaging and prayer requests. — [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/); [ChurchTechToday 2017](https://churchtechtoday.com/chms-review-acs-realm/)

### Inferences
- Targeting by group, serving team, tag or custom field is done by building a saved search or list, then messaging it. None of the products was documented as offering native WhatsApp. This matters for an Indonesian church.

### Gaps
- No detailed SMS or email docs were found for Realm or CCB (e.g. per-campus sender identity, opt-out handling).

## Q4. Multi-campus support and per-campus permissions

### Takeaway
CCB and Realm have explicit campus-scoped admin permissions or tiers (Realm's Multiply plan; CCB's campus-scoped Process Admin). PCO has a campus field on people and lists, but the sources found did not show export or view restrictions by campus. Breeze is widely described as weak for multi-site churches.

### Cited Findings
- PCO: lists can be filtered and organized by campus, and automations can update the primary campus. The "Edit campus" permission belongs to managers and editors. No source showed campus-restricted visibility or export. — [PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people); [PCO help: permissions in People](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/permissions-in-people.html) (search snippet)
- CCB: a Process Admin can create and edit processes only on campuses where they hold Process permission. Campus-level admins are part of the permission model. Multi-campus churches see which campus each process runs on. — [Pushpay support: Process Queues](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/Getting-Started-Process-Queues) (search snippet)
- Realm: the Multiply plan targets multi-campus churches and churches with plants. Profiles can be organized by campus. One review cites multi-campus management with shared or separated data. — [ChurchTechToday 2017](https://churchtechtoday.com/chms-review-acs-realm/); [Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/); [ChurchMemberPro Realm review](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/acs-realm-review/) (search snippet)
- Breeze "lacks advanced tools for managing multiple campuses". — [TheLeadPastor Breeze review](https://theleadpastor.com/tools/breeze-church-management-review/) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- For a multi-branch denomination like GRII, CCB's and Realm's campus-scoped admin roles are the relevant model. PCO's campus is mostly a label used for filtering.

### Gaps
- Realm's per-campus data separation rules were not documented in the sources found. The ChurchMemberPro Realm review fetch failed mid-response.

## Q5. What reviewers praise and complain about

### Takeaway
- PCO: praised for usability and Services. The main complaint is that cost adds up across separately billed apps.
- Breeze: praised as the easiest and cheapest. Complaints are about reporting, volunteer scheduling, integrations and multi-site.
- CCB/Pushpay: praised for depth and process queues. Complaints are about complexity, a dated UI, admin burden, and Pushpay sales and support.
- Realm: praised for accounting integration and depth. Complaints are about cost, learning curve, and paid training (2017 data).

No Reddit r/churchtech threads surfaced in searches; the `site:reddit.com` queries returned nothing.

**Realm** (2017; older)
- Pros:
  - accounting linked to membership and giving
  - Pathways for discipleship
  - filterable reporting
  - Connect app included
  - support included in the price
- Cons:
  - $39 advanced training webinars
  - costly for small churches
  - attendance and accounting take time to learn
  - hard for staff who are uncomfortable with computers
  - giving needs a separate merchant account — [ChurchTechToday Realm review 2017](https://churchtechtoday.com/chms-review-acs-realm/)

### Inferences
- The recurring trade-off is depth versus ease. Breeze wins on simplicity and price. CCB and Realm win on process, permissions and accounting depth, but need dedicated admin staff. PCO sits between them, with the best usability, at a cost that grows with the ministry.
