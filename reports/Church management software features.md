# Household registers win, family trees stay unbuilt

No church management system (ChMS) found anywhere in this research gives GRII the combination it needs. That combination is a household-first register that knows the Indonesian church vocabulary (Kartu Keluarga Jemaat, baptis, sidi, atestasi), scopes data by branch, reaches members over WhatsApp, and runs in Indonesian, English and both forms of Chinese. Indonesian products split into cheap secretary-register SaaS (Gerejaku.id, e-Gereja, GerejaSoft) and agency-built member apps for megachurches (Shiftsoft for GBI, GMS, GRII Pusat). The first group has no member-facing depth and the second has no documented register. The global leaders (Planning Center, Breeze/Tithely, Pushpay ChMS, Realm, ChurchSuite, Elvanto, Rock RMS) have mature membership patterns worth copying: households that own the address, a typed relationship list for kin outside the household, staff-locked identity fields, an opt-in per-field directory, export as its own permission, and saved rule-based lists for targeting. None of them supports WhatsApp natively, and none has a real family tree. GRII's Membership module should therefore copy the proven household-plus-relationships model and the consent-driven directory. It should add Indonesian specifics (KK card, sacrament dates, atestasi as an event, branch transfer on one record). It should compete on multilingual UI, phone-first design and WhatsApp, which no product found offers together. It should leave out a family-tree view, a report builder and approval queues for v1.

## Indonesia has three tiers, and none covers both the register and the member

The Indonesian market splits by who the product serves. **Local SaaS products serve the church secretary.** Gerejaku.id (CV. ASFA Solution, Cirebon, launched September 2023) stores individual records with Klasis/Sektor, citizenship, education, work, marriage and atestasi. It also prints a "Kartu Keluarga Jemaat" with head of household, tracks Pengurus/Majelis/Pendeta by term, and lets admins rename the territorial hierarchy. Prices run from free (100 members) to **Rp359.000/month above 500 members** ([Gerejaku.id](https://gerejaku.id/)). e-Gereja adds finance, e-bulletin, assets and committees, from free (50 members) to Rp500.000/month for an Enterprise tier that includes a member mobile app ([e-Gereja](https://www.e-gereja.com/)). GerejaSoft prints KAJ/KKJ member and family cards and certificates, supports custom fields, takes attendance by QR, RFID or biometrics, and schedules ministry over WhatsApp ([GerejaSoft](https://www.gerejasoft.com/about.php)). gerejaDigital.id offers a similar register with RFID attendance ([gerejaDigital.id](https://gerejadigital.id/)).

**Agency apps serve the member.** Shiftsoft builds native apps for GBI churches and GPIA SMCC. These include a digital kartu jemaat, worship and komsel attendance, warta with push, giving through DANA and LinkAja, penggembalaan (pastoral follow-up), activity points, and **"WA Blast" from the church's own WhatsApp number** ([Shiftsoft](https://shiftsoft.id/)). GBI Bethel's Shiftsoft app lists branches in Bandung, Kelapa Gading, Kedoya, Bekasi and Tasikmalaya. That branch list is the only visible multi-branch feature; no branch data model is documented ([Google Play: GBI Bethel](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.shiftsoft.gbibethel&hl=en_US)). GMS Church centers on schedules, event registration and growth milestones ([App Store: GMS Church](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1494423842)). The **GRII Pusat iOS app** (Jiosdev LLC, July 2025) already does event and Sunday-service registration with QR entry, profile editing and **family members managed under one account**. It had fewer than 1,000 downloads and no ratings ([mwm.ai: GRII Pusat](https://mwm.ai/apps/grii-pusat/6748234472)). GRII Sydney's app is media-only ([App Store: GRII Sydney](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1525471552)).

A third tier is **thesis-built single-congregation systems** (GMIT, HKI, several HKBP). These consistently list the same processes to computerize: member records, baptism, sidi, marriage, atestasi in and out, deaths and family data ([Dinamika repository](https://repository.dinamika.ac.id/id/eprint/1175/2/BAB_I.pdf), [Undana TekMas](https://ejurnal.undana.ac.id/index.php/TekMas/article/view/14227)). They exist because congregations ran on Excel, Word or paper. HKBP Cengkareng still registered members on paper in 2024 ([ITN JATI](https://ejournal.itn.ac.id/jati/article/download/18724/9867/)), and other churches required registration, baptism and sidi forms to be handed to the secretary offline ([UPJ eprints](https://eprints.upj.ac.id/id/eprint/8678/8/BAB%20I.pdf)).

| Product | Serves | Membership features | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gerejaku.id | Secretary | Individual + KK record, atestasi, office-holder terms, birthday reports, renamable hierarchy | Local vocabulary, Rp0–359k/month | No WhatsApp, export or member app advertised |
| e-Gereja | Secretary, treasurer | Membership, finance, e-bulletin, committees | Broad admin scope, free tier | Security described only as "aman" |
| GerejaSoft | Secretary, ministry heads | KAJ/KKJ cards, custom fields, QR/RFID attendance, WhatsApp scheduling | Most complete local feature list | Vendor-only claims, no reviews found |
| Shiftsoft (GBI apps) | Members | Digital card, komsel attendance, warta, giving, WA Blast | WhatsApp from church's number, e-wallets | Register depth and branch model undocumented |
| GRII Pusat app | Members | Registration with QR, profile, family under one login | Already GRII's member habit | Low adoption, no register behind it found |

The gaps matter as much as the features. **No Indonesian product advertised Chinese or any multilingual UI**, including those used by Chinese-heritage churches such as GKY and GRII. No product documents NIK storage, a digital atestasi letter between branches, branch-to-branch transfer, a member directory or CSV export. Security claims are vague. These are inferences from absence on marketing pages, and no Play Store review text could be retrieved to confirm them. Elsewhere in Asia the picture is thin. Hong Kong has ITChurch (iGears) ([iGears](https://igears.net/eng/it_church)), and Singapore churches run on global SaaS plus the locally hosted Sojourner platform, chosen to meet PDPA rules ([CMC Trellis](https://www.cmc.org.sg/trellis/)). No Korean, Taiwanese, Malaysian or Philippine product was identified.

## North American incumbents trade depth for ease, and price accordingly

The four big North American products show the main design trade-off. **Planning Center (PCO)** gives every church a free, unlimited People database. Services, Check-Ins, Groups, Giving, Registrations and Calendar are sold separately, each metered by its own usage ([PCO pricing](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing)). Reviewers rate it highly (Capterra **4.7/5 from 1,137 reviews**). The steady complaint is that per-app costs "add up quickly" and that the separately bought apps do not always fit together ([Capterra PCO](https://capterra.com/p/76708/Planning-Center/reviews/), [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/accounting/planning-center-profile/reviews/)). **Breeze** is one flat **$72/month** plan with unlimited people, directory, forms, tags, follow-ups and free export ([Breeze pricing](https://www.breezechms.com/pricing)). It is praised as the easiest to learn and criticized for weak reporting and multi-site support ([TheLeadPastor](https://theleadpastor.com/tools/breeze-church-management-review/)), and it is being folded into the Tithely brand. **Pushpay ChMS (formerly CCB)** is quote-based, aimed at churches with 500+ attendance, and strong on process queues and permissions. Reviewers call it "so robust you almost need a dedicated position" to run it, with an "outdated" UX ([Capterra CCB](https://www.capterra.co.za/reviews/9753/church-community-builder), [ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/ccb-church-community-builder-review/)). **ACS Realm** is the only one with built-in fund accounting and payroll. It has two apps, Connect for members and Shepherd for pastors, and a Multiply plan for multi-campus churches ([Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/), [ChurchTechToday](https://churchtechtoday.com/chms-review-acs-realm/)).

| Product | Pricing model | Best fit | Praised for | Criticized for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Planning Center | Free People + metered apps | Any size wanting modules | Usability, Services planning | Cost across apps, mobile app gaps, report learning curve |
| Breeze / Tithely | Flat $72/month | Small churches | Simplicity, price, free export | Reporting, multi-site, integrations |
| Pushpay ChMS (CCB) | Quote, annual contract | 500+ attendance | Process queues, permissions | Complexity, dated UX, sales/support |
| Realm | Tiered/quote | Mid–large, multi-campus | Accounting + membership in one | Cost, learning curve (2017 data) |

For multi-branch GRII, the relevant models are CCB and Realm. In CCB a Process Admin can act only on campuses where they hold permission ([Pushpay support](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/Getting-Started-Process-Queues)). PCO treats campus mostly as a label for filtering lists, and no source showed campus-restricted view or export. All four communicate by email and SMS. **None was documented as offering native WhatsApp**, and CCB offers email and US SMS only ([ChurchMemberPro](https://churchmemberpro.com/blog/ccb-church-community-builder-review/)).

## UK, Australian and open-source products set the bar for privacy and data modelling

Outside North America, three products stand out for membership design. **ChurchSuite (UK)** prices by module and contact band, from £9/month ([ChurchSuite pricing](https://churchsuite.com/pricing)). It has the strongest data-protection tooling found. Members choose which contact details other members see, every email has an unsubscribe, public forms show the privacy notice with explicit opt-in, consent requests are sent and evidenced, and a right-to-be-forgotten request notifies the church's data protection contact ([ChurchSuite GDPR](https://churchsuite.com/gdpr)). It also documents multi-site most clearly: **up to 15 sites under one account at no extra cost**, sharing settings, privacy notice and data protection contact ([ChurchSuite multi-site](https://support.churchsuite.com/article/216-multi-site-functionality)). That is close to GRII's central-admin, branch-scoped model. Its "key dates" (First Visit, Baptism, Wedding) and smart tags are cheap features with high pastoral value ([Address Book tour](https://churchsuite.com/tour/address-book)).

**Elvanto (Australia, now Tithely)** bills only active adults. It defines an active adult as someone who "attends your services, volunteers or gives financially", and children, contacts and archived people are free ([Elvanto pricing](http://www.elvanto.com/pricing/)). Its families use fixed roles (Primary Contact, Spouse, Child, Sibling, Partner, Other) and "support immediate family structures best" ([Elvanto Families](https://help.elvanto.com/hc/en-us/articles/7481201498903-Working-With-Families)). Reviewers report hard onboarding and stalled development since the acquisition ([Capterra Elvanto](https://www.capterra.com/p/125780/Elvanto/reviews/)).

**Rock RMS** is free, open source and self-hosted ([Rock self-host](https://www.rockrms.com/self-host)). It has the richest people model: families are groups, a person can belong to several, addresses belong to the family, and "known relationships" form a typed, two-way graph ([Rock Intro to People](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/church-management/people/overview/intro-to-people)). It scores **4.9/5 on Capterra**, but a database administrator called it hard to navigate with documentation that is often lacking ([Capterra Rock](https://capterra.com/p/141654/Rock-RMS/reviews/)).

Two others round out the field. **ChMeetings** has flat tiers from free (50 people) to $60/month unlimited. Its profiles link out to WhatsApp, Telegram and Viber ([ChMeetings pricing](https://www.chmeetings.com/pricing), [ZoftwareHub](https://zoftwarehub.com/products/chmeetings/features)), and real WhatsApp Business messaging comes only through a Saysimple integration ([Saysimple](https://www.saysimple.com/integrations/chmeetings)). **Subsplash** is app-and-media-first. It bundles ChMS into quote-priced Subsplash One, with a multi-campus app only in Enterprise ([Subsplash pricing](https://www.subsplash.com/pricing)).

## Membership design has converged on six patterns GRII can copy

### Households own the address; a relationship list covers everyone else

The industry standard is a hybrid. A household owns the address and mailing contact, a person can belong to several households with one marked primary, and a separate typed relationship list links kin outside the household. In PCO, people "can belong to multiple households" (for example, children of divorced parents). Each household has a primary contact used for mailing labels, and parents or guardians can edit members, register them and check them in ([PCO Households](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/138577-households.html)). Rock adds Known Relationships such as Grandparent, Invited By and Allow check-in by, and creating one automatically creates its inverse ([Rock docs](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/BookContent/5/44)). Realm's relationship tags show on both profiles and can be queried ("all grandparents"), but have no tree view ([Realm relationship tags](https://help.acst.com/en/realm/how-to/profiles/track-relationships/relationship-tags)). PCO has no relationship field at all and tells churches to use a custom field ([PCO custom fields](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/common-uses-for-custom-fields.html)). **No mainstream product models a real family tree.** That is a possible differentiator, but no source shows churches asking for one.

Products handle the hard cases with guided workflows, not free editing. Realm ships Separate Household, Get Married and Change Household. When a child who belongs to several families becomes an adult, they are removed from all but the primary family ([Realm life changes](https://help.acst.com/en/realm/how-to/profiles/life-changes/scenarios)). Touchpoint calls a married child moving out a "split" ([Touchpoint](https://docs.touchpointsoftware.com/People/SplitPersonFromFamily.html)). Churchteams keeps a "Related To" link to the family of origin ([Churchteams](https://help.churchteams.com/knowledge/remove-or-add-family-members)). Rock records "Previous Spouse" after a death and "Former Spouse" after a divorce. Single-household-only systems forced duplicate profiles for children of divorced parents ([PowerChurch forum](https://www.powerchurch.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=17489)). Family positions also carry permissions. In Realm, Head and Spouse can edit family members' details and privacy, Child and Other cannot, and a person is emailed when a relative changes their privacy ([Realm: your family](https://help.acst.com/realm/how-to/profiles/manage-your-information/your-family)). Locally, Gerejaku.id's head-of-household KK record and GRII Pusat's "family under one account" confirm that Indonesian members already expect this model.

### Membership status and record status are separate, and duplicates are the chronic cost

Rock separates connection status (Member, Attendee, Visitor, Participant, Pending) from record status (active or inactive, with a reason). It also stores previous names such as maiden names for matching ([Rock docs](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/BookContent/5/44)). Every vendor ships merge tooling, and merges are typically **irreversible**. Subsplash's "cannot be undone" ([Subsplash merge](https://intercom.help/subsplash/en/articles/9021418-people-merge)), and PowerChurch advises a backup first ([PowerChurch](https://www.powerchurch.com/support/456/5/powerchurch-software-newsletter-summer-2017)). Rock scores likely duplicates at entry (name 15+15, mobile or email 15, previous name 12, birth month and day 10, and so on), flags a score above 35, and lets users without edit rights file merge requests. Catching duplicates when a record is created is cheaper than merging later. Transfers show the opposite risk. In ParishSOFT the new parish creates a new record and the old one keeps an inactive copy ([ParishSOFT](https://support.parishsoft.com/hc/en-us/articles/14636365356059)). That is the right model across separate databases and the wrong one inside a single multi-branch system like GRII's, where it would create duplicates. Sacraments are dated person fields or milestones: Realm tracks Catholic and Protestant sacraments ([Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)), and ChurchSuite uses key dates. For a Reformed church, baptism, sidi and membership dates are core records that need a certificate attachment.

### Members edit their own contact details; staff lock identity

Leading products control self-service with locked fields, not approval queues. In PCO, members edit photo, marital status and similar fields in Church Center but not their name or any field an admin locks. Birthday and grade are admin-only, and a change submitted while logged out must be confirmed by email ([PCO profiles](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/138575-help-people-update-their-profiles.html)). Each Church Center field can be editable, editable only when blank, or view-only. Realm lets staff lock chosen fields ([Realm Profiles](https://www.acstechnologies.com/realm/tools/profiles/)). Churchteams offers an approval queue, but it is off by default ([Churchteams](https://help.churchteams.com/knowledge/how-does-churchteams-request-members-to-update-their-information-themselves)). Intake runs through forms that create "pending" people and feed follow-up workflows: PCO Workflows, CCB Process Queues, Realm Pathways, Elvanto People Flows and Rock Connections ([PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people), [Pushpay Process Queues](https://support.pushpay.com/s/article/Getting-Started-Process-Queues)). One Elvanto church reviews visitors after 6 attendances and requires approval from the pastor and an elder for member access ([Hills Bible Church](https://hillsbiblechurch.elvanto.com.au/file/3bd29621-41ff-4623-b166-61e90604d04f)).

### The directory is opt-in, per field, and set by the member

The modern directory is invite-only and consent-driven. In PCO, members "choose exactly what personal and household information they would like to share". "Remove all" opts out the whole household, and a privacy mode lets members contact each other without exposing email addresses ([PCO directory](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/141288-share-information-with-people-in-the-directory.html)). Realm is the richest: per-field audiences (whole church, my groups, leaders, staff only), whole-church visibility unavailable for under-18s, and defaults set by member status ([Realm: your family](https://help.acst.com/realm/how-to/profiles/manage-your-information/your-family)). CCB hides children below an age threshold from everyone except the primary contact, spouse, admins and approved child workers ([CCB glossary](http://designccb.s3.amazonaws.com/helpdesk/files/official_docs/glossaryofterms.pdf)). ChurchSuite asks on first login which details, if any, other members may see ([St Paul's guide](https://stpaulsletchworth.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/St-Pauls-General-Data-Protection-2019.pdf)). One church notice describes the visibility as reciprocal: details are shown only to members who also agreed to be listed ([Sidcup Baptist](https://www.sidcupbaptistchurch.org.uk/site_files/5710/upload_files/ChurchSuiteUseNoticeFeb2026.pdf?dl=1)). Usability is the known weak spot: a PCO reviewer said the mobile directory confuses members who are not tech-savvy ([Capterra PCO](https://capterra.com/p/76708/Planning-Center/reviews/?page=9)). That matters for GRII's older members.

### Export became its own permission because the old default leaked data

PCO changed course in early 2025. Before then, anyone with People access could export. **"Can export CSVs and reports" became its own permission**, and it is off for new users ([PCO blog](https://www.planningcenter.com/blog/2025/02/5-new-features-you-may-have-missed-in-people)). Private custom fields export only for people with access to them ([PCO export](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/export-data.html)). In the EU, GDPR treats church membership as special-category data. Article 9(2)(d) lets a religious body process it for its members and regular contacts, but bars disclosure outside the body without consent ([GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)). That makes export the main risk point. Indonesia's UU PDP, fully in force since October 2024, is stricter on different data. **Religion is general data, but child data and personal financial data are specific data.** Processing specific data is high-risk and can require a DPIA, and processing it at scale can require a DPO ([Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/perbedaan-jenis-data-spesifik-vs-umum-dalam-uu-pelindungan-data-pribadi-lt633f548091bfe), [detik](https://inet.detik.com/law-and-policy/d-7597590/uu-pdp-sudah-berlaku-ini-dampak-pelanggar-kebocoran-data)). A GRII register holding children in households and NIK is therefore in high-risk territory. No church-specific Indonesian guidance or religious exemption was found, and this is not legal advice.

### Targeting runs on saved lists, and WhatsApp is the hole in every global product

Every product targets messages through saved, rule-based segments: PCO Lists (all/any/none rules, filterable by campus, messageable by email, text or app announcement) ([PCO People](https://www.planningcenter.com/people)), Breeze tags, CCB saved searches, ChurchSuite smart tags and Elvanto People Views. PCO's lists refresh in batches, which creates friction: automations run only on refresh, and lists that depend on other lists must be refreshed in creation order ([PCO Lists](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139197-lists-overview.html)). Filters evaluated at query time avoid this. The household primary contact drives household-level mailings. In Indonesia, outbound communication is WhatsApp-first, through Shiftsoft's WA Blast and GerejaSoft's WhatsApp scheduling, while email is barely mentioned ([Shiftsoft](https://shiftsoft.id/), [GerejaSoft](https://www.gerejasoft.com/about.php)). No source compared the official WhatsApp Business API with unofficial blast tools or covered ban risk.

## A scoped Membership module for GRII

The evidence supports a v1 that copies the converged patterns, adds the Indonesian records, and leaves out features that only big-church incumbents carry. The table maps each candidate feature to its use and a recommendation.

| Feature | What it is used for | Evidence | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Person + household with role (head, spouse, child, other adult), household-owned address | KK-style family record; adults acting for members without accounts | PCO, Rock, Realm, Gerejaku.id, GRII Pusat | **v1** |
| Membership in several households, one primary | Shared custody; multi-generation homes | PCO, Rock | **v1** (cheap in schema, painful to add later) |
| Typed two-way relationships (grandparent, guardian, family of origin, invited by) | Kin outside the household; check-in rights | Rock, Realm, Churchteams | **v1** table; no tree view |
| Life-change actions (marry, split household, death, transfer branch) | Prevent stale households and duplicate records | Realm, Touchpoint, ChurchTrac | **v1** for marry/split/death; branch transfer as an event on one record |
| Membership status separate from record status; dated baptis, sidi, nikah, membership, atestasi in/out with attachment | Sacramental register; pastoral milestones | Rock, Realm, ChurchSuite, Indonesian theses | **v1** |
| Duplicate check at create (name, previous name, phone, birthdate) + audited merge | Avoid the chronic merge cost | Rock, Subsplash, PowerChurch | **v1** check; merge can follow |
| Self-edit of contact fields; identity fields staff-locked; "request change" for locked fields | Keep register accurate without approval queues | PCO, Realm, Churchteams | **v1** locks; request-change can follow |
| Onboarding form/QR creating pending person + follow-up steps | Visitor to member pipeline | PCO, CCB, Elvanto, GRII Pusat QR habit | **v1** pending + simple steps; no workflow engine |
| Opt-in, per-field, branch-scoped directory; minors hidden; household opt-out | Member-to-member contact with consent | PCO, Realm, CCB, ChurchSuite | **v1**, large-text phone UI |
| Export as separate branch-scoped permission, audited, NIK/notes excluded by default | UU PDP and leak risk | PCO 2025 change, GDPR Art. 9, UU PDP | **v1** |
| Saved filters (branch, wilayah, ministry, status, age) for targeting; per-channel consent (WhatsApp, email) | Announcements by location or ministry | PCO Lists, ChurchSuite tags, Shiftsoft | **v1** filters; WhatsApp sending belongs to the communications module |
| Printable KK and member card | Local expectation | Gerejaku.id, GerejaSoft, Shiftsoft | **v1** digital card; print later |
| Renamable territorial hierarchy (klasis/sektor/wilayah) | Branch-specific vocabulary | Gerejaku.id | Later, if branches differ |
| Family-tree view, report builder, approval queues, background checks | US megachurch needs | Reviewers complain about report builders | **Skip**; use fixed canned reports |

For reporting, reviewers of PCO and Breeze complain that building custom reports is hard ([Capterra Breeze](https://www.capterra.com/p/132513/Breeze-ChMS/reviews/?page=10), [Capterra PCO](https://capterra.com/p/76708/Planning-Center/reviews/?page=9)). Vendors advertise attendance trends and lapsed-attender detection ([GetApp Church360](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2080679/church360deg-members)). A fixed set of canned reports is the better fit: members by status and branch over time, new members, upcoming birthdays and anniversaries (which Gerejaku.id already offers), and baptism and sidi counts per year. No survey data shows which reports churches actually use, so this recommendation rests on complaints, not usage.

## Conclusion

GRII's advantage does not come from membership features. The global products have refined those for a decade, and copying their household, relationship, lock, directory and export patterns is low-risk. The advantage comes from the combination no product found offers: the Indonesian sacramental and KK register, real branch scoping inside one record (so a transfer is an event, not a copy), WhatsApp-first contact, four UI languages, and a phone-first member app usable by older members. The mobile app is the most consistent complaint about the market leader. Two warnings come from the incumbents' own history. PCO's late move to an export permission shows that permissive data access defaults get reversed, so GRII should start strict. CCB's "need a dedicated position" reviews show that configurable depth turns into admin burden for a volunteer-heavy church, so fixed workflows and canned reports should beat configurability.

The largest uncertainties are on the Indonesian side. No app-store reviews were retrieved, it is not known what GRII uses internally today, there is no data on how often blended or multi-household families occur in GRII congregations, and it is unclear how the UU PDP supervisory agency will enforce the rules on child data. Interviews with GRII branch secretaries about current records (Excel, paper or the GRII Pusat app) would test the scope above faster than more desk research.
